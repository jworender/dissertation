# ODU Dissertation Style Inconsistency Review

Reviewed document: `main.pdf` in this folder, compiled June 1, 2026.

Comparator set: the six ODU dissertation PDFs in `C:\Users\jason\ODU\dissertation_examples`.

All page references below use the visible printed page number in the PDF, not the PDF viewer's physical page index.

## Comparator Pattern

The examples are not stylistically identical, so "correct" should be interpreted by comparator type:

- For empirical computer-science chapters, the closest examples are the CS dissertations. They usually move from motivation/objectives to methods, then results, then discussion/summary/conclusion.
- For theorem and proof presentation, the closest examples are the math dissertations. They usually state lemmas/theorems formally, place core proofs in the chapter sequence, and reserve appendices for extended calculations.
- Where CS and math examples differ, the dissertation should follow the CS examples for empirical RQ1/RQ3 chapters and the math examples for the RQ2 proof-heavy chapter.

## Non-Issues Or Lower-Priority Differences

- The absence of a List of Algorithms does not appear inconsistent. The CS examples with algorithms also do not show a separate List of Algorithms in the front matter.
- The broad front/back matter order is consistent with the examples: table of contents, lists of tables/figures, chapters, references, optional appendices, and vita.
- Separate Future Work before Conclusion is consistent with the CS examples. Some math examples combine conclusions and future work, so this is a discipline-dependent variation rather than a problem.
- Keeping research questions in the introduction is consistent with the CS examples. The current source has an unused `Chapters/04_resquestions.tex`, but the compiled dissertation itself has research questions in Chapter 1, which is not a style inconsistency in the PDF.

## Priority

Highest priority: fix the RQ1 float backlog and the RQ2 proof format. These are the two issues most likely to affect committee readability: RQ1 because evidence appears after the answer, and RQ2 because central theoretical claims are not presented with the formal proof style used by the math comparators.

Second priority: move RQ3 motivation earlier and standardize RQ chapter endings. These are navigation improvements that would make the dissertation feel more consistent with the CS examples.
